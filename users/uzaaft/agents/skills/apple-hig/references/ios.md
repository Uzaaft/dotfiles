# Designing for iOS

Source: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ios

## Platform characteristics

1. Display: medium-size, high-resolution iPhone display.
2. Ergonomics: one-hand or two-hand use with frequent portrait and landscape changes at short viewing distances.
3. Inputs: Multi-Touch, virtual keyboard, voice, sensors, and personal-data powered flows.
4. App sessions: short check-ins and long immersive sessions both matter.

## iOS system features to account for

1. Widgets
2. Home Screen quick actions
3. Spotlight
4. Shortcuts and Siri flows
5. Activity views

## Best-practice checklist

1. Keep primary tasks obvious and reduce control clutter.
2. Make secondary actions discoverable with low interaction cost.
3. Adapt cleanly to orientation, Dark Mode, and Dynamic Type.
4. Place frequently used controls within comfortable thumb reach.
5. Support swipe-based back navigation and list-row actions where appropriate.
6. Integrate platform capabilities with permission-aware data use, such as location and biometrics.

## Implementation notes

1. Prioritize standard navigation and control components.
2. Validate touch-target sizes and spacing across compact and regular environments.
3. Ensure feature entry points are easy to reach in one-handed usage scenarios.
